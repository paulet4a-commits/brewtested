### Short 2 — "Flat vs conical, actually"
```
Hook (0-3s):
"Flat burrs vs conical burrs: the taste difference is real, but not what most people describe."

Body (3-45s):
"The common claim: flat burrs = brighter and more clarity. Conical = sweeter and rounder.
This is roughly true, but the more important difference is:

Flat burrs grind to a more uniform particle size.
This means more even extraction.
Flavor becomes more distinct — good AND bad.

Conical burrs produce a bimodal distribution — some fine particles, some coarse.
The fines fill gaps, can improve body, can mask sourness.
More forgiving of less-precise brewing technique.

For espresso specifically: flat burrs reward precision. Conical burrs forgive imprecision.

If you're 6+ months in and dialing in consistently: flat burrs will show you more of what the coffee is doing.
If you're still learning: conical is more forgiving while you develop technique."
```

## Reddit Helpful-Answer Drafts (3)

### Answer 1 — "Best espresso grinder for $200-300?"
**Target:** r/espresso grinder threads

> At $150-300, these are the grinders worth considering:
>
> **Eureka Mignon Specialità (~$185)** — Best overall under $200. Flat 55mm burrs, near-silent motor, under 0.2g retention, stepless adjustment. Italian build quality. The quiet motor alone justifies the price if you grind in the morning.
>
> **DF64 Gen 2 (~$130)** — Best value flat burr. 64mm flat burrs at this price is unusual. Near-zero retention, single-dose design. Trade-off: quality control variance, requires bellows, not beginner-friendly workflow.
>
> **Baratza Encore ESP (~$170)** — Best starting point if you want reliability. Conical, designed for espresso range specifically, Baratza's US repair support, good warranty.
>
> **Baratza Sette 270 (~$320, slightly over)** — Best if pulling 2+ shots daily and want precision. 270 micro-steps, 4s grind, direct portafilter. Worth stretching budget for if you're past the beginner stage.

### Answer 2 — "Is the DF64 Gen 2 worth it at $130?"
**Target:** r/espresso value/budget discussions

> Yes, with caveats.
>
> The DF64 Gen 2 gets you 64mm flat burrs — a burr size that normally costs $300-400. At $130, that's genuinely unusual. The flavor profile (bright, clarity-forward, good for light roasts) and near-zero retention for single-dosing are real advantages.
>
> The caveats:
> - Quality control varies unit-to-unit. Check burr alignment on arrival. Some units are well-aligned; some aren't.
> - Workflow is more involved: bellows to clear retention, WDT tool to prevent channeling.
> - The dial-in is sensitive — finer than most grinders at this price. Steep learning curve if you're coming from a stepped grinder.
> - Build quality is functional but not premium.
>
> If you're comfortable returning a unit if alignment is off, and fine with a more hands-on workflow: excellent value. If you want it to work perfectly out of the box: Eureka Mignon Specialità for $55 more.

### Answer 3 — "Eureka Mignon Specialità vs Baratza Encore ESP — which is worth the extra $15?"
**Target:** r/espresso comparison threads

> The Eureka Mignon Specialità is worth the extra $15 if any of these apply:
>
> - **You grind early morning**: The Eureka is nearly silent. The Encore ESP is noticeably louder.
> - **You switch beans often**: The Eureka's retention is under 0.2g. The Encore ESP is 0.5-1g. Lower retention means less stale cross-contamination between beans.
> - **You want flat-burr flavor profile**: The Eureka's 55mm flat burrs produce brighter, more clarity-focused espresso. Different from the Encore's conical.
> - **You plan to keep the grinder long-term**: Italian build quality, better long-term durability.
>
> The Encore ESP wins if: you want US-based repair support (Baratza's service is good), you're a beginner who wants a proven reliable starting point, or the $15 genuinely matters right now.
>
> For most buyers: the Eureka Mignon Specialità is the better under-$200 grinder. The quiet motor and low retention are worth the slight premium.
