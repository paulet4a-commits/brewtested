French press is the most forgiving immersion brewing method — no paper filters, no precise pour rates, just coarsely ground coffee steeped in hot water. Done right, it produces the richest, most full-bodied cup of any manual brewer. Here's how to get it right, plus the five presses worth owning.

## The Perfect French Press Recipe

| Variable | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Coffee | 30g (about 4 tablespoons) |
| Water | 500ml at 200°F |
| Grind | Coarse (sea salt texture) |
| Steep time | 4 minutes |
| Yield | 2 cups |

**The method:**
1. Preheat the press with hot water for 30 seconds — pour out
2. Add coarsely ground coffee
3. Start timer, pour all water at once — stir gently
4. Place lid (plunger up) — don't press yet
5. At 4 minutes — press slowly and steadily over 20-30 seconds
6. Pour immediately — don't let coffee sit (it keeps extracting)

## French Press Brewing FAQ

**Why is my French press coffee bitter?**
Over-extraction — usually from too-fine a grind or too-long a steep. Use coarse grind (like sea salt), keep steep to 4 minutes, and pour immediately after pressing. Don't let pressed coffee sit in the carafe.

**Why does my French press coffee taste weak?**
Under-extraction — usually from too-coarse a grind, too-cool water, or too-little coffee. Use 60g of coffee per liter, water at 200°F, and confirm your grind is coarse but not chunky (coarser than sea salt usually extracts too weakly).

**Do I need to pre-heat a French press?**
Yes — preheating with hot water before brewing prevents the carafe from absorbing heat from your brew water, maintaining extraction temperature throughout the 4-minute steep.

**Why is there so much sediment in my French press?**
Standard French press mesh filters allow fine coffee particles through — this is normal. To reduce sediment: 1) use a coarser grind, 2) press slowly, 3) let the cup sit 30 seconds before drinking (sediment settles), or 4) upgrade to an Espro P6 with a micro-filter.

**Can I use pre-ground coffee in a French press?**
Yes — but coarse pre-ground coffee is ideal. Most grocery store pre-ground is too fine for French press, leading to bitterness and clogged plunger. "French press grind" is a setting on most grinders.
