Single-origin coffee is where most home brewers unlock the real magic. Unlike blends that prioritize consistency, single-origin beans showcase the unique flavors of their terroir—the exact region, altitude, and processing method that makes Ethiopian Yirgacheffe taste completely different from Kenyan AA or Colombian Geisha.

We've spent the last year testing single-origin beans from roasters across four continents, brewing them through pour-overs, French presses, and espresso to understand which origins shine brightest in home brewing. Here's what we found works best.

## Single-Origin Tasting Guide

### Ethiopian Coffees
- **Profile:** Floral, fruity, tea-like, bright acidity
- **Best Roasts:** Light to medium
- **Ideal Method:** Pour-over
- **Flavor Keywords:** Blueberry, jasmine, bergamot, wine
- **Learning Value:** HIGH - teaches you acidity and floral complexity

### Kenyan Coffees
- **Profile:** Wine-like, structured, complex acidity
- **Best Roasts:** Medium
- **Ideal Method:** Pour-over, espresso
- **Flavor Keywords:** Blackberry, wine, stone fruit, citrus
- **Learning Value:** VERY HIGH - most complex single-origins

### Central/South American
- **Profile:** Balanced, chocolate, nuts, sweet
- **Best Roasts:** Medium to dark
- **Ideal Method:** French press, espresso, milk drinks
- **Flavor Keywords:** Chocolate, almond, caramel, balanced
- **Learning Value:** MEDIUM - good starting point, less challenging

### Indonesian Coffees
- **Profile:** Earthy, full-bodied, herbal, low acidity
- **Best Roasts:** Dark
- **Ideal Method:** French press, espresso
- **Flavor Keywords:** Earthy, cedar, mushroom, spice
- **Learning Value:** MEDIUM - teaches you wet-hulled processing differences

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What's the difference between single-origin and blend?**
A: Single-origin = one country/farm/lot. Blends mix origins for consistency. Single-origins show geographic personality; blends prioritize a consistent house flavor. Both are valid—single-origins teach you coffee diversity.

**Q: Which single-origin is best for beginners?**
A: Start with Ethiopian or Colombian (medium roast). They're forgiving, approachable, and show clear flavor characteristics. Avoid Indonesian dark roasts (too polarizing) until you understand your taste.

**Q: Should I buy whole bean or pre-ground?**
A: Always whole bean. Pre-ground loses aromatics within 15 minutes. Whole beans stay fresh 2-4 weeks post-roast if stored in an airtight container away from light.

**Q: How does altitude affect flavor?**
A: Higher altitude = slower ripening = more complex sugars = more interesting flavors. High-elevation coffees (5000-6500ft) generally have brighter acidity. This is why specialty roasters emphasize elevation.

**Q: What's a good price for single-origin coffee?**
A: $12-18/lb is specialty quality. Below $10 is usually blend territory. Above $25/lb is premium micro-lots (nice but not necessary). Sweet spot is $14-16/lb for excellent quality.
