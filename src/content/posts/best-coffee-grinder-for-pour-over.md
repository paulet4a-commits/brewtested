## FAQ: Common Questions About Pour Over Grinders

**Q: What grind size should I use for pour over?**
A: Pour over typically calls for medium grind—similar to sea salt texture. Too fine and water flows too slowly; too coarse and extraction is weak. Test and adjust based on brew time (aim for 3–4 minutes).

**Q: Do I really need a burr grinder for pour over?**
A: Yes. Blade grinders produce inconsistent particle sizes, causing uneven extraction. Burr grinders (conical or flat) cut grounds uniformly, which is essential for pour over's direct contact brewing.

**Q: What's the difference between conical and flat burrs?**
A: Conical burrs are more forgiving with temperature control and work across a wider range. Flat burrs deliver exceptional consistency in specific grind ranges but run hotter. For pour over, either works well—conical is more versatile, flat is more precise.

**Q: Can I use an espresso grinder for pour over?**
A: Yes, but it's not ideal. Espresso grinders excel at fine grounds and have many micro-settings for that range. Most lack granularity in the medium range pour over needs. You'll have fewer adjustment options.

**Q: How often should I clean my grinder?**
A: Clean the burr chamber weekly if grinding daily. Use a dry brush or compressed air to remove grounds residue. Some grinders (like Baratza Encore) have removable burrs for thorough cleaning monthly.
