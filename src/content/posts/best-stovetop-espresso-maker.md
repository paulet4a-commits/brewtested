## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much coffee does a 6-cup moka pot actually make?
A 6-cup moka pot brews about 9 ounces (roughly 3 demitasse cups or 1.5 standard coffee mugs). The "6-cup" measurement refers to traditional Italian demitasse cup size, not standard US coffee cups.

### Can I use regular drip coffee in a moka pot?
Technically yes, but don't. Moka pots work best with coffee ground specifically for espresso or moka pot brewing—medium-fine, consistent grind. Regular drip grind is too coarse and produces weak, underwhelming coffee. [Check out our guide to the best espresso beans under $20](/posts/best-espresso-beans-under-20/) to get the right coffee for your brewer.

### Do I need a special burner for a moka pot?
Only if you want induction compatibility. Classic aluminum moka pots require gas or electric coil burners. If you have an induction cooktop, choose one of the stainless steel models (Venus, Cuisinox Roma, Grosche Milano, or Bialetti Moka Induction).

### How long does a moka pot last?
Aluminum models last 10-15 years with basic care. Stainless steel models often last 20+ years. The most common failure point is the gasket (rubber seal), which typically lasts 3-5 years before needing replacement. Most manufacturers sell replacement gaskets for $5-10.

### Is a moka pot actually espresso?
Not technically. True espresso requires 9+ bars of pressure. A moka pot generates only 1-2 bars, producing "stovetop espresso"—stronger and richer than drip coffee but not true espresso. However, it's the closest you'll get without a $300+ machine, and many coffee lovers genuinely prefer the taste.
