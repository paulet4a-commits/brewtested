## Introduction

Stepless adjustment coffee grinders are the key to dialing in the perfect extraction for any brewing method. Unlike stepped grinders that jump between preset coarseness levels, stepless grinders offer infinitely variable settings—letting you make micro-adjustments to dial in espresso shots or fine-tune your pour-over consistency. We've researched and compared the top stepless grinders on the market to find models that deliver precision, durability, and reliability without breaking the bank.

## FAQ

**Q: What's the difference between stepless and stepped grinders?**
Stepped grinders jump between 15-40 preset grind sizes, making fine adjustments impossible. Stepless grinders offer infinite positions between coarse and fine, letting you dial in the exact texture needed for your brew method. Stepless is superior for espresso and specialty coffee.

**Q: Can I use a stepless grinder for both espresso and pour-over?**
Yes, most stepless grinders handle both, though some are optimized for one or the other. Flat-burr grinders (like Wilfa and Fellow Ode) excel at both. Conical-burr grinders (Baratza, Timemore) work well for both but achieve slightly better results when specialized.

**Q: How often should I replace the burrs?**
Conical and flat burrs typically last 300-500kg of beans before noticeable wear. For home use (30g/day), that's 3-5 years. Manual grinders generally outlast electric ones since there's no motor stress. Most manufacturers offer affordable burr replacement kits.

**Q: Do I need a stepless grinder, or will a stepped grinder work?**
For espresso, stepless is nearly mandatory—micro-adjustments between shots are essential. For filter coffee (pour-over, Chemex, drip), a quality stepped grinder works perfectly. Stepless offers flexibility but isn't required for filter methods.

**Q: What grind size should I start with?**
- French press: coarse (like breadcrumbs)
- Pour-over: medium (like sea salt)
- Espresso: fine (like flour)
- Moka pot: medium-fine (like powdered sugar)

Most grinder dials mark these zones—start there and adjust from feedback (extraction time, taste).
