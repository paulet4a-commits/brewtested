## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why does a coffee scale matter?**

Coffee extraction depends on the ratio of coffee to water. Most people use volume (tablespoons, scoops) which varies wildly based on grind size and how tightly you pack grounds. A scale removes that variable. The standard ratio is 1:16 (1g coffee to 16g water), and hitting that target consistently is what separates good coffee from inconsistent coffee.

**What accuracy do I actually need?**

For pour over and drip: 1g accuracy is plenty. For French press and cold brew: 1g accuracy works fine. For espresso: 0.1g accuracy makes a real difference, especially at lower doses (18-20g shots).

**Do I need a timer function?**

Not necessary. A phone timer works fine. But a dedicated timer eliminates one distraction during brewing, so if you do pour overs regularly, it's worth the convenience.

**Can I use a regular kitchen scale for coffee?**

Yes, absolutely. Any scale with a tare function and 1g accuracy works for brewing. Dedicated coffee scales add features (timers, faster response) but aren't mandatory.

**What's the best scale for espresso?**

If you're serious about espresso, the Acaia Pearl Model S is worth the investment. If you want 0.1g accuracy at a lower price, the Hario V60 works perfectly fine for espresso too—it's just marketed for pour over. For casual espresso (using a stovetop Moka pot or Flair), the Jennings CJ4000 is more than adequate.

**How do I calibrate a coffee scale?**

Most coffee scales come with calibration weights or instructions. Check your manual. For regular use, recalibrate monthly on a surface that's completely level. Place the scale on wood or stone, not on fabric or uneven counters.
