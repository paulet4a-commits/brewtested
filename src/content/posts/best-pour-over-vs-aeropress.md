## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Which method produces better coffee?**

Both produce excellent coffee when executed properly. Pour overs typically deliver brighter, cleaner cups with more complexity; AeroPress delivers fuller-bodied, more forgiving cups. Quality depends more on bean freshness, grind consistency, and water quality than method choice.

**Q: Can beginners use pour over successfully?**

Yes, but it requires more attention to technique. Pour over success depends on water temperature (195-205°F), pour rate, and water distribution. AeroPress is more beginner-friendly because its design compensates for minor technique variations.

**Q: Is AeroPress worth it over cheaper pour overs?**

If you travel frequently, make coffee daily, or value consistency, the $30-40 AeroPress investment pays for itself within months through improved brewing success rates. For casual brewers, a $20 Hario V60 might suffice.

**Q: How many brews can you get from one AeroPress filter?**

Standard micro-filters are single-use (one brew per filter). You can reuse them 2-3 times before flavor degradation, though most brewers prefer fresh filters. Annual filter cost is $10-15 for daily brewing.

**Q: Can you brew multiple cups with either method?**

Pour overs scale easily—use a larger dripper and carafe to brew 3-4 cups. AeroPress is designed for 1-2 cup maximum; brewing larger volumes requires multiple brew cycles.

**Q: What grind size should I use?**

Pour over: Medium-fine (like sea salt). AeroPress: Medium (like sand). Both require burr grinders for consistency; blade grinders produce uneven particle size leading to inconsistent extraction.
