## Introduction

1. **Heat Retention** — Filled each press with 200°F water and measured temperature loss at 30 minutes, 1 hour, 2 hours, and 4 hours. Tracked how long coffee remained above 140°F (drinkable threshold).

## FAQ

**Q: How long does an insulated French press keep coffee hot?**
Double-wall stainless steel models keep coffee above 140°F (hot enough to drink) for 2-4 hours depending on design and ambient temperature. Vacuum-sealed models (Fellow, Planetary Design) consistently reach 4+ hours. Regular glass presses lose heat in 30-45 minutes.

**Q: Why is my insulated French press still cold?**
Most likely causes: (1) the thermal seal has degraded, (2) you didn't preheat the press before brewing, or (3) the model prioritizes coffee quality over insulation. Preheating with hot water for 1 minute before adding coffee extends retention by 30+ minutes.

**Q: Can I use an insulated French press for cold brew?**
Yes, the insulation works both ways—it keeps cold coffee cold too. Brew at room temperature for 12-24 hours, then serve cold or reheat carefully. The double-wall prevents condensation on the exterior.

**Q: What's the difference between stainless steel and vacuum insulation?**
Double-wall stainless steel has air trapped between two metal layers, providing good insulation ($30-70 models). Vacuum-sealed removes most air from the gap, providing superior insulation ($100-220 models). Vacuum seals eventually degrade; stainless steel lasts forever.

**Q: Should I buy an insulated press or use a thermal carafe?**
Insulated presses are better for single-brew sessions and taste (full immersion steeping). Thermal carafes are better for keeping large batches hot all day. For morning coffee, an insulated press is ideal.

**Q: How do I clean an insulated French press?**
1. Empty grounds and dispose (compost if possible)
2. Rinse with hot water
3. Fill halfway with hot water + dish soap, plunge 5-10 times
4. Rinse thoroughly until no soap residue
5. Dry immediately to prevent water spots
6. Never put insulated models in the dishwasher—heat damages seals
