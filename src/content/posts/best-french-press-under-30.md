## How to Brew French Press Coffee (The Right Way)

Most people under-extract their French press coffee. Here's the method that works:

### What you need
- Coarsely ground coffee (sea salt texture)
- Water just off boil (200°F / 93°C)
- 4-minute steep time
- 1:15 ratio (1g coffee per 15ml water)

### Step by step

1. **Preheat the press** — pour hot water in, swirl, dump. This prevents the carafe from absorbing heat from your brew water.

2. **Add grounds** — for a 34oz press, use 22g (about 3.5 tablespoons) of coarse ground coffee.

3. **Bloom first** — pour just enough water to saturate the grounds (about 50ml). Wait 30 seconds. This releases CO2 and improves extraction.

4. **Fill and stir** — pour the remaining water and stir gently. Put the lid on (don't press yet).

5. **Steep 4 minutes** — set a timer. Under 3 minutes = weak. Over 5 minutes = bitter.

6. **Press slowly** — take 20–30 seconds to press down. Fast pressing stirs up sediment.

7. **Pour immediately** — don't leave coffee in the press. It continues extracting and turns bitter.

## FAQ

**Why is my French press coffee bitter?**

Three most common causes:
1. Water too hot (use 200°F, not boiling)
2. Steep time too long (over 5 minutes)
3. Grind too fine (use coarse — like sea salt)

**Why is my French press coffee weak or sour?**

The opposite: grind too coarse, steep time too short (under 3 minutes), or water temperature too low.

**How do I clean a French press?**

Disassemble the filter completely after each use. Coffee oils build up inside the mesh and turn rancid. Wash with warm soapy water. Once a week, soak the filter in a baking soda solution for 30 minutes.

**Can I use pre-ground coffee in a French press?**

Yes, but most pre-ground coffee is too fine for French press and will produce muddy, bitter results. Look for coffee specifically labeled "French press grind" or "coarse grind," or grind your own beans.

**How long does a French press last?**

The glass on a Bodum Chambord typically lasts 2–5 years with normal use (it's glass — drops break it). The stainless steel options (SterlingPro, Coffee Gator) can last a decade or more. The filter meshes on all of them are replaceable.
