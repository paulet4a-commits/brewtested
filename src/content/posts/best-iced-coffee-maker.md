## Iced Coffee FAQ

**What's the difference between cold brew and iced coffee?**
Iced coffee: hot-brewed coffee poured over ice (or brewed at double-strength directly over ice). Fast to make, can taste diluted if not concentrated. Cold brew: coffee grounds steeped in cold water for 12-24 hours — produces a smoother, less acidic, naturally sweet concentrate. Both are delicious; the method determines the flavor profile.

**How do I prevent watery iced coffee?**
Two options: 1) Brew hot-over-ice with a dedicated machine (Cuisinart) that double-concentrates, or 2) Make cold brew concentrate and dilute with just a small amount of ice. Never pour standard-strength drip coffee over regular ice — the ice melts too fast.

**How long does cold brew last in the fridge?**
Cold brew concentrate: up to 2 weeks refrigerated. Diluted cold brew: 3-4 days. Hot-brew iced coffee: same day only — it oxidizes quickly.

**What coffee should I use for cold brew?**
Medium to dark roast. Coarsely ground (like French press). Coarser grind = less bitter extraction over 12-24 hours. Light roasts can work but produce a more floral, acidic result — less typical for cold brew.

**What's the best coffee-to-water ratio for cold brew?**
For concentrate: 1:5 (1 cup coffee to 5 cups water) — dilute 1:1 with water or milk before serving. For ready-to-drink: 1:8 or 1:10.
