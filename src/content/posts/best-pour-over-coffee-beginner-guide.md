## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What water temperature should I use for pour-over?**
A: Aim for 195-205°F (90-96°C). Boil water and let it cool for 30 seconds, or use a thermometer. Too hot over-extracts (bitter), too cool under-extracts (sour). This single variable impacts flavor more than anything except grind size.

**Q: How much coffee should I use?**
A: Start with a 1:16 coffee-to-water ratio. That's 1 gram coffee to 16 grams water (or 1:2 cups by volume in a pinch). A scale is optional but transforms consistency. Without one, experiment with 1 tablespoon per cup until you find your preference.

**Q: Do I need special filters?**
A: Standard #2 cone filters work with most drippers. Some designs (Chemex, Kalita Wave) benefit from their proprietary filters, but standard ones still work fine. Paper filters produce cleaner cups than cloth; start with paper.

**Q: How long should brewing take?**
A: Most pour-overs finish in 2.5-4 minutes. Too fast (under 2 min) means coarser grounds or hot water. Too slow (over 5 min) means finer grounds or cooler water. Time guides dial-in but shouldn't become rigid—cup taste is the real goal.

**Q: Can I make pour-over coffee without a scale?**
A: Yes, but consistency suffers. A simple digital scale ($10) transforms your results. If skipping the scale, use a consistent scoop and time to dial in repeatable recipes.
