## FAQ

**Q: Can I use any filters with any pour over dripper?**
A: Not all filters are interchangeable. Hario V60 uses V60-specific cone filters, CHEMEX requires CHEMEX filters, and Melitta drippers need Melitta cone filters. That said, universal flat-bottom filters work with cone-shaped drippers in a pinch, though fit isn't perfect. Check your dripper before buying filters.

**Q: How much should I spend on a starter pour over coffee set?**
A: You can start quality manual brewing at $15-30 for complete beginner kits. Once you know you enjoy the ritual, upgrading to a $50+ kit like CHEMEX pays dividends in longevity and aesthetic appeal. Think of it as investing in daily ritual, not just coffee equipment.

**Q: Do I really need a special kettle for pour over?**
A: A gooseneck kettle (with a narrow spout) gives you much better pouring control than a standard kettle, reducing splashing and over-saturation. It's not absolutely required, but it dramatically improves the experience. See our [best pour over kettle guide](/posts/best-pour-over-kettle/) for affordable options.

**Q: How long do paper filters last in storage?**
A: Paper filters last 2-3 years unopened in a cool, dry place. Once opened, aim to use them within 6-12 months before they begin absorbing odors from your cabinet. Store in an airtight container if possible.

**Q: What's the difference between a Chemex and a V60?**
A: CHEMEX uses thicker paper filters that produce cleaner, brighter coffee with lower sediment, while brewing in larger batches (3-6 cups). V60 produces slightly richer, fuller-bodied coffee due to thinner filters and faster extraction, making it better for single-serve brewing. See our detailed [Chemex vs pour over comparison](/posts/best-chemex-coffee-maker/) for more nuance.
