## Milk Frother FAQ

**What's the difference between a frother and a steamer?**
A steam wand (found on espresso machines) injects steam directly into milk, creating true microfoam through aeration + heat. An electric frother uses a whisk disc to spin air into milk while heating. Steam wands produce better microfoam; frothers are more accessible and convenient.

**Does milk type affect frothing?**
Yes significantly. Whole milk produces the densest, creamiest foam. 2% milk froths well but with slightly less body. Oat milk froths almost as well as whole milk (look for "barista edition" oat milks — they're formulated for frothing). Almond milk is the hardest to froth well.

**What temperature should frothed milk be?**
140-160°F (60-71°C) is optimal for lattes and cappuccinos. Above 170°F, milk proteins denature and the flavor becomes flat. The Breville Milk Café is the only frother on this list with adjustable temperature.

**Can I use a frother for cold foam?**
Yes — the Aeroccino 4, Instant Pot frother, and Breville all have dedicated cold foam modes. Cold foam uses the same frother without heat — creating thick, dense foam for cold brews and iced lattes.

**How do I clean a milk frother?**
Rinse immediately after use before milk dries. For jug frothers: fill with water, run one empty cycle, wipe. Most discs are dishwasher safe. Never submerge the motor base.
