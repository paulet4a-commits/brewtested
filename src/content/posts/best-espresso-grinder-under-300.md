## Buying Guide: Choosing the Right Espresso Grinder

### Burr Type: Conical vs. Flat

**Conical burrs** (Fellow Opus, Breville) rotate at lower speeds, reducing heat and static. They excel with lighter roasts and complement pour-over brewing as a bonus. Best if: You prioritize flavor and occasionally grind filter coffee.

**Flat burrs** (Sette 270, Eureka, Rancilio) generate more particles of identical size, critical for reproducible espresso shots. They're louder and hotter but deliver the precision serious home baristas demand. Best if: Dialing in is your weekly ritual and you want surgical precision.

### Adjustment Type: Stepless vs. Stepped

**Stepless adjustment** (Fellow Opus) offers infinite micro-adjustments, ideal for dialing in with every single-origin bean. Can feel overwhelming—you'll pull 5-8 trial shots per dial.

**Stepped adjustment** (Sette 270's 270 micro-steps, Eureka's 11 steps) balances precision with decision clarity. Fewer decisions per dial-in session. Best for most home users.

### Hopper Size

- **35g (Sette 270):** Single-dose control, minimal stale grounds, best for rotating beans
- **110g (Fellow, Eureka, Rocky):** Family-friendly, one refill per morning
- **120g (Breville):** Maximizes convenience without bulk

### Noise Level

Early-morning espresso? The Eureka Silenzio (65dB) operates 20dB quieter than others. Flat-burr grinders average 80-85dB; conical designs hit 75-80dB.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use an espresso grinder for filter coffee?**  
A: Yes, but flat-burr espresso grinders produce too-fine distributions for optimal filter extraction. Conical designs (Fellow Opus, Breville) work acceptably for pour-over. For dedicated filter + espresso use, consider the Baratza Encore (separate grinder, under $50).

**Q: How often should I dial in espresso?**  
A: Every time you change beans (at minimum weekly for rotating single-origins). Temperature, humidity, and bean age shift the grind size needed. Research shows baristas achieve best flavor with 2-3 dial-in shots per new bean.

**Q: What's "grind by weight" vs. "grind by time"?**  
A: Grind-by-weight (Sette 270 with scale) delivers repeatable doses (e.g., 18g always). Grind-by-time uses a timer, easier but less precise as beans age. The Sette 270 and Fellow Opus support weight-based workflows.

**Q: Does grinder speed matter for espresso?**  
A: Yes. Low RPM (Fellow's 150 RPM) reduces static and heat, preserving acid and floral notes in light roasts. High RPM flat burrs (Breville, Rancilio ~1400 RPM) work fine with darker roasts.

**Q: Should I spend $300 or $160 for my first espresso grinder?**  
A: Start at $160-195 (Breville or Fellow). If you're dialing in more than weekly, upgrade to the Sette 270 ($300). The jump to $800+ grinders rewards 5+ espresso shots per day, not home hobbyists.
