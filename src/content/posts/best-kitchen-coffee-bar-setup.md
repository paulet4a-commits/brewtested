## Frequently Asked Questions

**What's the minimum budget for a quality home espresso setup?**
You can start with a Gaggia Classic Pro ($100-150) and Baratza Encore grinder ($40-50) for about $150-200 total. This combination produces genuinely good espresso. Add a scale when you're ready to dial in properly—it makes an enormous difference in consistency.

**Can I make good espresso without an expensive machine?**
Absolutely. The Gaggia Classic Pro proves that $150 machines produce café-quality espresso. Your skills and technique matter more than machine price. Focus on a good grinder first, then add shot accuracy tools like a scale and tamper.

**Should I buy a machine with a built-in grinder?**
No. Integrated grinders are almost always poor quality, making shot consistency nearly impossible. Invest in a standalone burr grinder—it's the single most important upgrade for espresso quality after the machine itself.

**What's the best milk frother for beginners?**
The Fellow Opus is ideal for beginners because it removes the learning curve of manual steam wands. Practice manual technique with a Gaggia or Rancilio if you want to develop true barista skills, but automatic frothers produce excellent results for drinking enjoyment.

**How often should I clean my espresso machine?**
Backflush your portafilter after every shot and soak removable parts in cleaning solution weekly. Full descaling is needed monthly if using hard water, less frequently with filtered water. A clean machine produces dramatically better tasting shots.
