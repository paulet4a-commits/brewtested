## FAQ: Common Questions About Espresso Cup Sets

**Q: What's the ideal espresso cup size?**
A: Traditional espresso cups hold 2.7–3.5 oz (demitasse standard). This concentrates heat and aroma while providing adequate volume for single or double shots. Larger cups (4–6 oz) work for cortados and flat whites but dilute the espresso experience.

**Q: Are double-wall espresso cups worth the cost?**
A: Yes, if you drink espresso regularly. Double-wall insulation lets you hold the cup comfortably immediately after pulling a shot, prevents external condensation, and keeps espresso at optimal temperature longer.

**Q: Can I use porcelain espresso cups in the microwave?**
A: Check the manufacturer, but most food-safe porcelain is microwave-safe. However, heating espresso in a microwave defeats the purpose—espresso is best fresh from the machine. Use microwave only for pre-warming cups with hot water.

**Q: How do I prevent espresso stains in porous cups?**
A: Glass cups resist staining naturally. Porcelain can absorb oils—wash immediately after use and occasionally soak in baking soda solution. Ceramic and stoneware benefit from frequent rinsing.

**Q: Can espresso cups work for other beverages?**
A: Absolutely. The small size works for Turkish coffee, small servings of dessert beverages, or even tea. Some sets (like Le Creuset) are versatile enough for souffle cups or small desserts.

**Q: Do expensive espresso cups taste better?**
A: Cups don't change flavor, but premium materials (thin-walled porcelain, borosilicate glass) enhance aroma perception and heat retention, making espresso taste subjectively better. The ritual and presentation also elevate the experience.
