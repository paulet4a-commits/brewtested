## How We Researched

We filled each thermos with freshly brewed coffee at 195°F and measured temperature retention at 1, 3, 6, 12, and 24-hour intervals using a digital thermometer. We researched and compared for:

- **Heat retention:** How long coffee remained above 150°F (still drinkable)
- **Leak resistance:** Carrying sealed thermoses in bags and vehicles
- **Ease of cleaning:** Accessibility of interior surfaces
- **Real-world durability:** Repeated opening, closing, and light drops
- **Lid performance:** Seal quality, spill prevention, ease of use

All tests were conducted at room temperature (70°F) with thermoses stored upright.

## FAQ

**Q: What temperature should coffee be to stay hot in a thermos?**
A: Freshly brewed coffee at 195-205°F will stay at drinkable temperatures (140°F+) for 6-12 hours in a quality thermos. Cooler coffee won't stay hot as long, so brew fresh for best results.

**Q: Can I put a thermos in the dishwasher?**
A: No—the vacuum seal can be damaged by high heat and pressure. Hand wash all vacuum flasks with warm soapy water and dry thoroughly.

**Q: Which thermos is best for camping?**
A: The Stanley Classic is ideal for camping—it's durable, keeps coffee hot for a full day, and survives the rough treatment of outdoor use.

**Q: Do thermoses keep cold drinks cold?**
A: Yes, all these models work equally well for cold beverages, keeping ice water cold for 12+ hours depending on the model.

**Q: Is a larger thermos always better?**
A: Not necessarily. Larger thermoses (1.1qt) are heavier and bulkier. For daily commutes, smaller options like the Contigo Pinnacle are more practical. Match size to your actual needs.
