## FAQ

**What's the best coffee maker if I'm just starting out?**

Start with either the Mr. Coffee ($20-30) for ultimate simplicity, or the Hamilton Beach ($40-55) if you want programmable convenience. Both are forgiving of beginner mistakes. Skip expensive machines until you've figured out your coffee preferences.

**Do I need a grinder if I buy these machines?**

No—these all use pre-ground coffee or pods. Once you get comfortable brewing, consider adding a grinder later (entry-level burr grinders start around $25). But don't let lack of a grinder stop you from starting.

**How much does it cost per cup with these machines?**

Drip machines: $0.05-0.15 per cup (coffee beans cost). Pod machines: $0.50-1.00 per cup (K-Cups). If budget is tight, skip pods and buy beans in bulk from Costco or local roasters.

**Can I make espresso with these machines?**

No—these are all drip/automatic machines. Real espresso requires 9+ bars of pressure. If espresso interests you, check out our [best espresso machines for beginners guide](/posts/best-espresso-at-home-beginners-guide/).

**How often should I clean my coffee maker?**

Daily: Rinse the filter basket and carafe. Weekly: Run water through an empty brew cycle. Monthly: Descale using white vinegar (especially in hard-water areas). Most of these machines have filters that prevent mineral buildup, but descaling extends their life.

**What if my coffee tastes bitter or weak?**

Bitter = too hot or over-extracted (brew time is long). Weak = not enough ground coffee or under-extraction. Start with a standard ratio: 1 tablespoon coffee per 6 ounces of water. Adjust from there based on taste.
