## What Makes a Burr Grinder Worth Buying?

**Burr vs blade:** Blade grinders chop unevenly — some particles are powder, some are chunks. Burr grinders crush beans between two abrasive surfaces, producing uniform particle size. Uniform grind = even extraction = better coffee.

**Burr material:** Stainless steel burrs are common at this price. Ceramic burrs run cooler but cost more. At sub-$100, steel burrs are fine.

**Grind settings:** More settings = more flexibility. Look for at least 12-16 settings for drip and pour over. Manual grinders typically have stepless adjustment (infinite).

**RPM:** Lower RPM = less heat = less flavor degradation. Under $100, this is hard to control — don't obsess over it.

### Should I buy a manual or electric?

**Manual:** Better grind quality per dollar. Quieter. Takes effort and time (1-2 min per cup). Great for AeroPress and pour over enthusiasts.

**Electric:** Convenient. Faster. More consistent at high volume. Better for daily drip coffee or households brewing multiple cups.

### Can a $100 grinder do espresso?

No. Espresso requires extremely fine, consistent grinds and precise stepless adjustment. Budget electric grinders (under $150) can't do this well. The Timemore C2 manual grinder can approach espresso territory with patience, but a dedicated espresso grinder starts at ~$200.

## FAQ

**Is a burr grinder worth it vs blade?**
Yes, if you care about coffee quality at all. A $25 burr grinder makes better coffee than a $60 blade grinder.

**How long do burr grinders last?**
With basic cleaning every 2-4 weeks, a quality burr grinder lasts 5-10 years. Budget models 2-4 years.

**Do I need to clean my grinder?**
Yes. Coffee oils go rancid. Clean burrs and chamber every 2-4 weeks with a brush or grinder cleaning tablets (Grindz, ~$8 on Amazon).

**Can I grind spices in a burr grinder?**
Don't. Spice oils contaminate the burrs and affect coffee flavor permanently. Use a dedicated blade grinder for spices.
