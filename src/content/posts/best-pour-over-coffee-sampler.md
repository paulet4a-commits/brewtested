## How We researched and compared Pour-Over Coffee Samplers

Our rigorous evaluation process included:

1. **Freshness Assessment** – Tracked roast dates and evaluated aroma intensity, bloom volume during brewing, and crema characteristics to measure coffee vitality.

2. **Flavor Profiling** – Brewed each sample using a Hario V60 dripper with filtered water at 200°F, timing extraction at 2:30-3:00 minutes, and cataloged actual flavor notes against tasting profiles.

3. **Consistency Testing** – Brewed each coffee 3 times over one week and again after two weeks to evaluate flavor stability and detect staling patterns.

4. **Grind Size Response** – Tested each bean at multiple grind settings to assess flexibility and forgiving nature for different brewing styles.

5. **Value Analysis** – Calculated cost per cup, compared against retail single-bag pricing, and evaluated sampler pricing relative to quality delivered.

6. **Variety Scoring** – Assessed roast range, origin diversity, and educational value of accompanying materials.

7. **Sustainability & Ethics** – Reviewed roaster sourcing practices, Fair Trade certifications, and farm relationship transparency.

## Brewing Tips for Pour-Over Coffee Samplers

**Water Temperature:** Heat water to 195-205°F. Coffee sampler beans are typically roasted for optimal extraction at this range. Too hot burns lighter roasts; too cool produces sour, under-extracted cups.

**Grind Size:** Use a medium grind (slightly coarser than sand) for most pour-overs. If extraction tastes bitter, go slightly coarser. If sour, grind finer.

**Water-to-Coffee Ratio:** Start with 1:16 (15g coffee to 240g water). This yields a balanced cup for most single-origins. Adjust up to 1:15 for lighter roasts if your cup tastes thin.

**Bloom Phase:** Wet grounds with 2-3x the coffee weight in water (30-50g), wait 30 seconds. This releases CO2 and ensures even extraction.

**Brewing Time:** Aim for 2:30-3:30 minutes total brew time from first water pour to final drip.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need a grinder to use coffee samplers?**  
A: Yes, unless you buy pre-ground samplers like Dripkit. Whole bean samplers require a burr grinder for best results. A quality conical burr grinder like the Baratza Encore ($45-60) pairs perfectly with any whole bean sampler.

**Q: How long does coffee stay fresh after roasting?**  
A: Peak flavor typically lasts 2-4 weeks post-roast. Subscription samplers roasted to order (like Trade) maximize this window. Retail samplers may be 1-2 weeks old, reducing the quality window.

**Q: What's the difference between a sampler and just buying full bags?**  
A: Samplers cost 20-30% more per ounce but eliminate the risk of purchasing a full bag you dislike. For $45-50, you sample 4 different roasters without committing $15-20 per bag. Once you find favorites, buy full bags directly from roasters.

**Q: Can I use sampler beans for espresso or French press?**  
A: Yes, though most samplers are optimized for pour-over. Light roasts from Onyx work well for espresso but require precision. Darker roasts work for French press but may taste flat. Trade and Atlas include notes on brew method versatility.

**Q: How often should I get a new sampler?**  
A: Quarterly subscriptions ($45-50 every 3 months) provide consistent discovery and freshness. Many roasters offer one-time purchases if you prefer flexibility.
