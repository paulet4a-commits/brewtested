## How We Tested Coffee Grinders

Our testing process for each grinder included:

1. **Grind Consistency** – We examined particle size distribution under magnification across 10 consecutive grinds at the same setting to measure uniformity and fines production.

2. **Heat Generation** – Using infrared thermometers, we measured temperature increase in the bean hopper and grounds container during continuous grinding.

3. **Noise Levels** – We recorded decibel readings during operation to assess daily usability in shared kitchens.

4. **Speed** – Timed grinding 30g of medium-roast beans from hopper to portafilter/cup.

5. **Durability** – Ran each grinder 500+ times over 4 weeks, checking for motor strain, burr wear, and consistency retention.

6. **Cleaning Ease** – Tested disassembly and cleaning requirements; measured static cling and bean oil residue.

7. **Real-World Brewing** – Used each grinder with the same beans across pour-over, French press, and espresso to evaluate actual flavor impact.

## Conical vs Flat Burr: The Science

**Conical Burr Advantages:**
- Lower heat generation during grinding
- Fewer ultra-fine particles (fines)
- Better for oils-forward beans (French press)
- More forgiving with varying bean densities

**Flat Burr Advantages:**
- Extremely uniform particle size
- Minimal fines for cleaner extractions
- Better particle density distribution
- Superior for filter coffee clarity

Neither type is objectively "better"—it depends on your brewing method. Pour-over and specialty coffee favor flat burrs. French press and espresso often prefer conical burrs' lower heat.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I really need a burr grinder, or is a blade grinder okay?**  
A: Burr grinders are essential if you care about extraction quality. Blade grinders produce inconsistent particle sizes, leading to uneven extraction, weak cups, and bitter notes. The $40-$60 investment in a basic burr grinder (like the Encore) pays for itself in better-tasting coffee within weeks.

**Q: Can I use a conical burr grinder for espresso?**  
A: Yes, but with caveats. Conical grinders like the Encore and Virtuoso work for espresso, though they generate slightly more heat on fine settings. For serious espresso, flat burr grinders or dedicated espresso grinders are better. If you only occasionally pull espresso shots, a conical grinder handles it fine.

**Q: Which burr type lasts longer?**  
A: When properly maintained, both conical and flat burrs last 500+ hours of grinding. Flat burrs slightly degrade uniformity over time, while conical burrs remain consistent longer. That said, quality determines longevity more than burr type. A well-built conical grinder (Baratza, Breville) outlasts a cheap flat burr model.

**Q: Why do some grinders create so much static?**  
A: Static cling occurs when beans lose moisture during grinding friction. Conical burrs generate less heat, so they produce slightly less static. To combat it, dampen your beans with a few drops of water using a spray bottle, or grind into a slightly damp portafilter.
