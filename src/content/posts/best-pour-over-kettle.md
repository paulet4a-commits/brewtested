## Pour Over Kettle FAQ

**Do I need a gooseneck kettle for pour over?**
Not strictly required — but highly recommended. A gooseneck gives you precise control over pour rate and where water lands on the grounds. This directly impacts extraction evenness and cup quality. A standard kettle pours too fast and can over-agitate the grounds.

**What temperature should pour over coffee water be?**
195-205°F (90-96°C). The SCA recommends this range for optimal extraction. Too hot (above 205°F) extracts bitter compounds. Too cool (below 190°F) under-extracts — flat, sour coffee.

**Do I need variable temperature?**
For coffee specifically — not essential. Coffee always uses water at 200°F. But if you also brew tea, variable temperature (160°F for green tea, 175°F for oolongs, 212°F for black tea) is very useful.

**How long does temperature hold matter?**
Hold function keeps water at the set temperature for 30-60 minutes. This is useful for bloom-and-wait pour methods (like Chemex) where the brew takes 4+ minutes. Without hold, your water cools and you end up pouring at below-optimal temperature.

**Stovetop vs electric gooseneck — which is better?**
Electric: faster, easier, more consistent. Stovetop: simpler, lasts longer, lower cost long-term. For most people, electric is more practical. For purists who enjoy the process, stovetop is satisfying.
