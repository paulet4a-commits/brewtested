## Why Grind Size Matters for French Press

French press brewing works by immersion: coffee grounds sit in hot water for 4 minutes, then you plunge to separate the grounds. Coarse grounds are essential because:

- **Prevents Over-Extraction:** Fine grounds over-extract during the long steep, making the coffee bitter
- **Stops Mud:** Coarse grounds don't pass through the metal filter, giving you clean coffee without sediment
- **Maximizes Clarity:** You taste the bean's origin and roast, not over-extracted harshness

The ideal French press grind is roughly the size of sea salt—chunky enough to not slip through the filter, but fine enough to extract well during the steep.

## How to Dial in Your French Press Grind

**Step 1:** Start on your grinder's coarser settings (usually settings 12-15 on a 40-setting grinder).

**Step 2:** Grind about 30g of beans and brew a cup.

**Step 3:** Taste the result. If bitter, go coarser. If weak and sour, go finer.

**Step 4:** Make one adjustment per cup until you hit that sweet spot.

**Step 5:** Once dialed in, use the same setting for that bean. Different beans may need slight tweaks.

Pro tip: If your grinder has markings or numbered settings, write them down for your favorite beans so you can dial back in faster next time.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use a blade grinder for French press?**
A: You can, but the uneven particle size will give you inconsistent results. Blade grinders heat the beans and can damage delicate flavors. Burr grinders are worth the investment.

**Q: How often should I replace my grinder burrs?**
A: With daily use, burrs last 500-1000 pounds of beans—typically 2-3 years. Most modern grinders have easily replaceable burrs (usually $15-30).

**Q: Does grind consistency really matter that much?**
A: Yes. Uneven grounds mean some particles over-extract while others under-extract. You lose the clean, flavorful cup that French press is known for.

**Q: Can I use the same grinder for French press and espresso?**
A: Yes, but you'll need a grinder with very fine settings. The Baratza Encore and Bodum Bistro both work for coarse French press and finer brew methods.

**Q: What's the difference between burr and blade grinders?**
A: Burr grinders crush beans between two surfaces to a consistent size. Blade grinders chop beans like a blender, producing uneven pieces. Burrs always win for consistency and heat control.
