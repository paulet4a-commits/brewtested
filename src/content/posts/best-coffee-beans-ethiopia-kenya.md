## Frequently Asked Questions

**What's the difference between Ethiopian and Kenyan coffees?**

Ethiopian coffees typically emphasize floral and fruity characteristics with lighter body, often featuring wild berry, jasmine, or wine-like notes. Kenyan coffees tend toward brighter acidity with bold fruit flavors (red currants, black currants) and cleaner finishes. Both are high-altitude origins that produce exceptional complexity.

**Is Ethiopian Yirgacheffe worth the price premium over regular Ethiopian blends?**

Absolutely, if you value flavor distinction. Yirgacheffe is a specific region within Ethiopia known for natural process fermentation that creates distinctive blueberry and floral notes. Blended Ethiopian coffees often use washed process beans from other regions. The $2-3 premium per pound is justified by the unique flavor profile.

**Can I use these single-origins in an espresso machine?**

Light-roasted Ethiopian and Kenyan beans can work in espresso, but they're not ideal. These origins shine in pour-over and immersion brewing methods that allow their delicate flavors to develop without the intensity and pressure of espresso extraction. Consider medium-roasted options if espresso is your primary brewing method.

**How should I store Ethiopian and Kenyan beans to maintain freshness?**

Store in an opaque, airtight container away from heat, light, and humidity. Room temperature is ideal—never refrigerate or freeze. Most whole beans stay fresh for 3-4 weeks after opening. Buy whole beans only (not pre-ground), and grind immediately before brewing.

**Which coffee is best for beginners?**

Start with either Keurig Green Mountain Ethiopian (convenience) or Volcanica Ethiopian Yirgacheffe (quality at beginner price). Both offer genuine single-origin Ethiopian experience without demanding advanced brewing skills. Once you develop your palate, graduate to the brighter Kenyans or more complex Yirgacheffe naturals.
