If you're pulling espresso at home, your grinder matters more than your machine—maybe even more than your beans. Get it wrong, and no amount of dial-twisting or tamping technique will save you.

## How We researched and compared

We evaluated each grinder across five criteria:

1. **Grind Fineness:** Can it grind fine enough for espresso extraction?

3. **Adjustment Precision:** How fine are the grind size steps? Can you dial in with confidence?

We pulled 30+ shots with each grinder using the same espresso machine (9-bar pump machine), same beans, and same tamping technique. We measured extraction time, visual consistency of the grind, and tasted every shot. Grinders that couldn't extract in 25–30 seconds or produced uneven extraction failed the espresso test.

## FAQ

### Why can't I use a regular grinder for espresso?

Espresso requires a grind about 200+ times finer than French press. Most general-purpose grinders (like blade grinders or budget conical burr grinders) have too much particle size variation at that fineness. You'll get a mix of powder and larger chunks, causing uneven water flow through the puck. Water channels through the larger particles while flowing slowly through powder, under-extracting some grounds and over-extracting others. Your shot tastes thin, sour, or bitter—sometimes all at once. A true espresso grinder maintains uniformity at that ultrafine size.

### What grind size should I use for espresso?

Espresso grind is about 0.5 to 0.75 millimeters—similar to fine sand or table salt. You'll know you're close when:
- Your shot extracts in 25–30 seconds (starting from water contact)
- The espresso flows smoothly, not gushing or trickling
- Taste is balanced (not sour or bitter)

Every grinder and bean combination is different. You'll adjust by making micro-changes, pulling a test shot, and tasting. It's normal to dial in 2–5 times before hitting the sweet spot.

### Stepped vs. stepless grinders—which is better?

**Stepped grinders** have preset positions (like "grind 3," "grind 4"). Adjustment is coarse but repeatable. You might jump over your ideal setting before finding it. Better for dialing in once and grinding the same beans for weeks.

**Stepless grinders** let you adjust anywhere along a spectrum. More granular control but harder to repeat the exact same setting days later. Better for single-dosing different beans frequently.

For home use, stepped grinders work fine. Stepless adds convenience you might not need.

### Should I buy a burr grinder or blade grinder?

Never buy a blade grinder for espresso. Blade grinders produce wildly inconsistent particle sizes. Your shot will choke, channel, or taste like dirt. Burr grinders (flat or conical) keep particles uniform. This is non-negotiable.

### Can I grind beans the night before?

Yes, but not ideal. Ground coffee absorbs moisture and oxidizes. Grind within 30 minutes of brewing for best flavor. If you grind ahead, store in an airtight container. Grinding fresh every morning is worth the 10 seconds of extra time.
