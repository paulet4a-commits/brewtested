## How We researched and compared

We evaluated each grinder across five criteria:

1. **Espresso Fineness:** Can it grind fine enough to pull proper espresso shots with 9 bars pressure? We measured particle size distribution using standard mesh comparisons.

2. **Consistency:** We pulled 10 consecutive shots on the same espresso machine with each grinder's factory settings. We evaluated shot times, crema, and taste notes to measure dial-in repeatability.

3. **Burr Quality:** We examined burr material, alignment, and wear patterns. Conical and flat burrs were tested equally; we focused on actual performance over theoretical advantages.

4. **Adjustability:** We rated the grind setting resolution and ease of making micro-adjustments between settings. This matters more for espresso than filter coffee.

5. **Real-World Use:** We ground beans with each grinder 15+ times, noting noise level, grind speed, ease of cleaning, and durability under regular use.

All testing was conducted with the same espresso machine (Gaggia Classic Pro), same beans (medium roast house blend), and same tamping technique to ensure apples-to-apples comparison.

## FAQ

**Q: Can you make espresso with a blade grinder?**
A: Technically yes, but it's frustrating. Blade grinders produce inconsistent particle sizes—some dust, some chunks—which causes uneven water flow during extraction. You'll get channeling, weak shots, and unpredictable results. A burr grinder, even a cheap one, is worth the investment.

**Q: Is a manual grinder practical for espresso?**
A: The JavaPresse is great for occasional espresso or travel. For daily espresso drinkers, an electric grinder saves time and effort. If you're pulling 1-2 shots per day, manual is perfectly viable. If it's 3+ shots, go electric.

**Q: Why does grind consistency matter for espresso?**
A: Espresso uses high pressure (9 bars) pushing water through tightly packed grounds. If particles vary wildly in size, small particles get over-extracted (bitter, channeling) while large particles under-extract (sour, weak). Consistency means even extraction and balanced flavor.

**Q: Can I use an espresso grinder for drip coffee or French press?**
A: Yes. Most of these grinders handle coarse grinds fine, though they're optimized for espresso. The Bodum BISTRO and Capresso Infinity are particularly versatile. The JavaPresse and Mr. Coffee are less flexible—they perform best at fine grinds.

**Q: How often do burrs need replacing?**
A: Quality burrs last 500+ hours of grinding (2-3 years for most home users). Cheaper burrs wear faster. All the grinders here have replaceable burrs available, though replacement costs vary.
