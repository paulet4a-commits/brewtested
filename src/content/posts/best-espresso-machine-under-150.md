## How We Researched

We evaluated each machine across five criteria:

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can you make good espresso under $150?**
A: Yes, absolutely. Machines like the Gaggia Classic Pro and Breville Bambino produce legitimate café-quality shots. The limitation is consistency and heat stability—but for daily drinking, sub-$150 machines are entirely viable.

**Q: Do I need a burr grinder with these machines?**
A: Yes. A proper burr grinder is non-negotiable. Espresso demands consistent, fine grind sizes that blade grinders cannot provide. Check out our [best espresso grinder under $100](/posts/best-espresso-grinder-under-100/) guide for paired recommendations.

**Q: What's the difference between 9-bar and 15-bar pressure?**
A: 15-bar machines extract shots faster with thicker crema. 9-bar machines require slightly longer extraction times but still produce excellent shots if you dial in your grind correctly. The difference matters less than fresh beans and proper tamping.

**Q: Should I buy used to save money?**
A: Used machines can be excellent value if sold by careful owners. Check heating element condition, test the pump, and ensure all seals are functional. Budget machines depreciate heavily, so you might find excellent deals at 40-50% off retail.

**Q: Can I upgrade these machines later?**
A: The Gaggia Classic Pro is the most upgradeable with community-driven modifications. The Breville Bambino has limited upgrade potential. De'Longhi and Mr. Coffee machines are designed for use-as-is without deep modifications.
