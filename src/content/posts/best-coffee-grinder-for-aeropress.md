The AeroPress is a brewing method that rewards precision. Get the grind wrong, and you'll either over-extract bitter coffee or under-extract a flat, sour cup. The difference between a mediocre cup and an exceptional one often comes down to one thing: your grinder.

Unlike some brewing methods that tolerate inconsistent grind sizes, the AeroPress demands uniformity. Research shows that burr grinders consistently outperform blade grinders by producing grind particles within a tighter range. For AeroPress specifically, you're targeting a medium-fine grind—finer than drip coffee, coarser than espresso.

Here are the five grinders that user feedback indicates work best with AeroPress.

## Why Burr Grinders Matter for AeroPress

Blade grinders smash beans into wildly inconsistent fragments—some powder-fine, some in large chunks. This creates two problems:

1. **Uneven extraction**: Fine particles over-extract and bitter. Coarse chunks under-extract and taste sour. You get both flavors at once.
2. **Grind loss**: Fine particles clog the AeroPress filter and cloud your cup.

Burr grinders (either flat or conical) produce particles of uniform size. This means more consistent extraction and cleaner, clearer coffee.

## Manual vs. Electric: Which Should You Choose?

**Manual grinders** (C3 Pro, Skerton Pro):
- Best if: You brew 1-2 cups daily, travel frequently, enjoy the ritual
- Trade-off: 2-3 minutes of grinding per cup

**Electric grinders** (Baratza Encore, Fellow Opus, OXO):
- Best if: You brew multiple cups daily, prioritize speed, want zero hands-on effort
- Trade-off: Counter space, noise, 50% higher cost

For AeroPress specifically, grind consistency matters more than grinder type. A $45 manual grinder will produce a better cup than a $50 blade grinder, every time.

## AeroPress Grind Setting Guide

Once you've chosen a grinder, dial in with this reference:

- **Too coarse** (settings 12+): Under-extracts, tastes watery/sour
- **Too fine** (settings 1-3): Over-extracts, tastes bitter
- **Sweet spot** (settings 6-8): Medium-fine, balanced, full-bodied

Most AeroPress users find their ideal setting between 6 and 8, regardless of which grinder they use. Start there and adjust by one setting at a time.
