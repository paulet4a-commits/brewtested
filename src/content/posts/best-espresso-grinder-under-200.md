## Frequently Asked Questions

**What's the difference between flat and conical burrs for espresso?**
Flat burrs cut grounds more uniformly (lower fines) but generate more heat and are noisier. Conical burrs shear grounds (slightly more fines) but run cooler and quieter. For espresso, flat burrs typically produce slightly better clarity; conical burrs are more forgiving.

**Do I need a $200+ grinder for good espresso?**
No. These sub-$200 grinders produce espresso indistinguishable from machines costing 2-3x more. The difference is features (automatic dosing, digital timers) not quality. A $139 Sette 270 produces better espresso than a $500 grinder with poor technique.

**How often do espresso grinder burrs need replacing?**
Quality flat burrs last 300-500 hours of grinding. Conical burrs last 500-800 hours. At 1 hour daily grinding, you're looking at 1-2 years before replacement ($40-80). Budget this as annual maintenance cost.

**Can I use an espresso grinder for drip coffee or pour-over?**
Yes, espresso grinders work for coarser grinds. However, you're "wasting" the micro-adjustment capability. Conversely, drip grinders are usually too coarse to dial in espresso shots properly. If you drink both, choose an espresso grinder—it handles everything better.

**What's the best way to adjust espresso grind settings?**
Traditional method: Pull a shot, time extraction. If under 25 seconds (too fast), grind finer. If over 30 seconds (too slow), grind coarser. Modern method: Use a scale to dose in (18-20g) and weigh output (36-40g). The scale method is faster and more accurate.
