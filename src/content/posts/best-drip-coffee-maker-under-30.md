## How We Tested

We evaluated each coffee maker across five criteria:

**Brew quality:** We used the same coffee (fresh, medium roast), same water temperature, same grind size, and same coffee-to-water ratio (1:16) with each machine. We tasted the coffee immediately after brewing and assessed for bitterness, balance, and clarity.

**Speed:** We timed how long each machine took from power-on to full carafe. All machines performed within the 6-10 minute window.

**Reliability:** We ran 20 full brew cycles with each machine over two weeks and looked for consistency in brew output, heat stability, and any mechanical issues.

**Heat retention:** We measured carafe temperature every 15 minutes for an hour after brewing to assess how long coffee remained palatable.

**Build quality:** We examined materials, seams, heating element durability, and overall construction for any obvious weak points.

All machines performed adequately, with differences coming down to features, capacity, and design preferences rather than catastrophic failures.

## FAQ

**Q: Do budget coffee makers brew worse coffee than expensive ones?**
A: Not necessarily. Under $30, all these machines use similar heating elements and brewing methods. The difference is usually features (programmable, thermal carafe), capacity, and durability—not the actual coffee quality. A $100 machine might last longer or brew more consistently, but the cup difference is subtle.

**Q: Which one makes the hottest coffee?**
A: The Proctor Silex tends to brew slightly hotter (around 203°F), and the Cuisinart with its thermal carafe keeps coffee hot the longest. Most others hover around 195-200°F, which is ideal for drip brewing but cools down quickly on the heating plate.

**Q: Can I use a reusable filter instead of paper?**
A: Yes, all five machines accept standard metal filters. Just rinse after each use. You'll get slightly more body and oils in the cup compared to paper filters, which some people prefer.

**Q: How long do budget coffee makers last?**
A: Typically 3-5 years of daily use. The simpler the machine (Proctor Silex), the longer it usually lasts because there are fewer components to fail. Programmable models (Hamilton Beach) sometimes have electronic failures sooner.

**Q: Is it worth upgrading if I already have one of these?**
A: Not unless it breaks or you hit a specific pain point (like hating bitter reheated coffee, which the Cuisinart solves). These machines do their job. Save the upgrade money for a better grinder, which has a bigger impact on coffee quality.
