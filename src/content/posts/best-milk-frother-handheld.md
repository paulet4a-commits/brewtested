## FAQ: Handheld Milk Frother Questions

**Can handheld frothers create latte art?**  
Yes, but it requires practice. The Zulay and Bonsenkitchen produce fine enough microfoam for basic latte art patterns. Coarser frothers (PowerLix, Aerolatte) won't work for detailed designs.

**Which milk froths best in handheld frothers?**  
User feedback indicates whole cow's milk froths easiest. Oat milk works well with stronger motors (Bonsenkitchen, PowerLix). Almond and soy are trickier—lower protein content resists frothing.

**How long do batteries last in handheld frothers?**  
Owner reviews report 2-3 months with daily use (1-2 lattes per day). Battery life varies by motor strength—higher-torque models drain faster.

**Can you froth cold milk in handheld frothers?**  
Yes, all battery-powered models work with cold milk. Manual models (Aerolatte) work but require more pump strokes. Automatic models like Aeroccino3 have a dedicated cold foam mode.

**Are handheld frothers quieter than espresso machine steam wands?**  
Much quieter. Owner reviews note handheld frothers operate around 70-75 dB—quiet enough for shared living spaces. Espresso machine steam wands are often louder.

**How do you clean a handheld milk frother?**  
Most use stainless steel removable whisks (dishwasher safe). Run under hot water immediately after use to prevent milk buildup. Some users fill a cup with hot soapy water and froth to self-clean.

**What's the difference between microfoam and regular foam?**  
Microfoam is fine, velvety, tiny bubbles ideal for lattes and latte art. Regular foam is thicker, coarser bubbles better for cappuccinos. Handheld frothers vary in which they produce best.

**Can handheld frothers heat milk?**  
Most battery-powered models don't include heating—you heat separately. PowerLix has optional heating mode. Automatic models like Aeroccino3 heat while frothing.
