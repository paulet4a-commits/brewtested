## Why Pour Over Coffee?

Pour over brewing offers unmatched control over extraction. Unlike drip machines or French press, you control water temperature, pour rate, and contact time—directly affecting flavor. The result: cleaner, brighter coffee that highlights the bean's origin characteristics.

If you're here, you're serious about your coffee. You've probably noticed that pre-ground coffee from a drip machine tastes flat, or that French press sediment bothers you. Pour over is the Goldilocks solution: precision without complexity.

## Complete Pour Over Brewing Guide

Success with pour over depends on three factors: water temperature, coffee-to-water ratio, and technique. Master these, and any brewer becomes excellent.

### Step 1: Grind Your Coffee
Use fresh beans ground immediately before brewing. Grind size matters—medium-fine works for most pour overs, but adjust based on your brewer and taste preferences.

**Recommended ratio:** 1 gram coffee to 17 grams water (1:17 ratio). For practical measurement: 1 tablespoon (about 7g) coffee per 120ml water.

### Step 2: Heat Water
Boil filtered water and let it cool to 195-205°F (90-96°C). Cold or boiling water produces extraction problems. An electric kettle with temperature control removes guesswork.

### Step 3: Prep Your Brewer
Place your filter in the dripper and rinse with hot water. This removes paper debris and preheats the brewer. If using paper filters, use white filters for lighter roasts and brown filters for darker roasts.

### Step 4: Add Coffee
Pour ground coffee into the filter, creating a slight well in the center. Level the bed gently.

### Step 5: Bloom Phase (First 30-45 seconds)
Pour just enough hot water to saturate the coffee grounds. This phase releases carbon dioxide and blooms the coffee. Use about 2x the weight of your coffee in water. Wait 30-45 seconds.

### Step 6: Main Pour (2-3 minutes)
Slowly pour the remaining water in circular motions. For a Hario V60 or Kalita Wave, pour in stages, maintaining a consistent water level. For Chemex, pour larger amounts less frequently due to the thicker filters. Aim for total brew time of 3-4 minutes.

### Step 7: Finish
When the last drops fall through (usually when water level reaches the coffee bed), stop pouring. Let the dripper finish naturally—don't squeeze out the final drops, which taste bitter.

## Pour Over vs. Other Brewing Methods

**Pour Over vs. Drip Coffee Machine:** Drip machines produce flat coffee from uneven heat and stale grounds. Pour overs give you complete control and fresher flavor.

**Pour Over vs. French Press:** French press produces heavier-bodied coffee with oils, some prefer this richness. Pour overs are cleaner and brighter. French press easier for beginners.

**Pour Over vs. AeroPress:** AeroPress is faster and more forgiving. Pour over requires technique but produces superior clarity, especially for single-origin beans.

**Pour Over vs. Moka Pot:** Moka pots produce espresso-like concentration. Pour overs suit filter coffee lovers who want brightness and origin character.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need a gooseneck kettle?**
Not essential, but highly recommended. A regular kettle works, but gooseneck design gives you the control that makes pour over special. A standard gooseneck kettle costs $20-30.

**Q: What type of water should I use?**
Filtered water is best. Tap water with high chlorine tastes off. Distilled water doesn't extract flavor fully. Use filtered tap water for optimal results.

**Q: How often should I replace my dripper?**
Ceramic and glass drippers last decades if handled carefully. Plastic components (like Oxo) last 3-5 years with regular use. You'll replace them long before they break from use.

**Q: Can I use any coffee for pour over?**
Technically yes, but pour over shines with fresh, quality beans. Pre-ground supermarket coffee tastes acceptable but won't showcase the brewing method. Invest in beans from a local roaster for the best experience.

**Q: How long can I leave water sitting in a pour over?**
Don't leave brewed coffee in any dripper longer than 30 minutes. It continues extracting and becomes over-extracted and bitter. Pour into your cup immediately after brewing finishes.
