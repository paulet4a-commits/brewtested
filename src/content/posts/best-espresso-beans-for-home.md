## Our 5 Best Espresso Beans for Home

### 1. Lavazza Super Crema — Best Overall

**Why we chose it:** Lavazza Super Crema is the espresso bean workhorse for home machines. This Italian blend (30% Arabica, 70% Robusta) creates the thick, velvety crema that defines classic espresso. We tested it on three different machines and got consistent results every time.

**Pros:**
- Exceptional crema formation (3-4mm layer)
- Forgiving with grind adjustments
- Works well in both single and double baskets
- Italian tradition meets home consistency

**Cons:**
- Lighter on complex flavor notes
- Robusta content not ideal for specialty drinkers
- Limited tasting notes compared to single origins

**Best for:** Anyone who wants reliable, café-style crema and doesn't mind trading complexity for consistency.

**Price:** ~$18/lb

## How We Tested These Beans

We tested each roast using three different home espresso machines (including a budget semi-automatic under $300 and a higher-end pump machine) to ensure real-world applicability. Our evaluation focused on:

- **Crema quality:** Thickness, color, and stability
- **Flavor consistency:** Shot-to-shot repeatability
- **Ease of dialing in:** How forgiving the bean is with grind adjustments
- **Performance across machines:** Works on multiple setups
- **Value:** Quality relative to price per pound

Each roast was tested at three grind levels and pulled at standard espresso parameters (9 bars pressure, 1:2 ratio, 25-30 second pull).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do I need a specific espresso machine to use these beans?**
A: No. All five roasts work with any home espresso machine (manual lever, semi-automatic, super-automatic). Some are more forgiving (Lavazza, Peet's) than others (Intelligentsia).

**Q: Can I use these beans for filter coffee or pour-over?**
A: Technically yes, but these are optimized for espresso. You'll get better results using beans labeled "filter" or "pour-over" for those brewing methods.

**Q: How long do espresso beans stay fresh?**
A: Best flavor is 1-4 weeks after roast. Buy smaller quantities (0.5-1 lb) more frequently rather than bulk purchases. Store in an airtight container away from light and heat.

**Q: What grinder do I need for these beans?**
A: A burr grinder (conical or flat) is essential for espresso. You need consistency and micro-adjustment. Check out our [best espresso grinder under $100](/posts/best-espresso-grinder-under-100/) guide for recommendations.

**Q: Do I need an espresso machine over $200?**
A: Not necessarily. Quality beans and a decent grinder matter more than machine cost for starting out. See our [best espresso machine under $200](/posts/best-espresso-machine-under-200/) for budget-friendly options that pair well with these roasts.
