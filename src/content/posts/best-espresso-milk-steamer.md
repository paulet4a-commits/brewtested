## FAQ: Espresso Milk Steamers

**Q: What's the difference between foam and microfoam?**
A: Foam is large bubbles you can see (cappuccino style). Microfoam is tiny, integrated bubbles that create velvety texture (latte style). Steamers that create silky texture rather than separated bubbles produce microfoam.

**Q: Can I use non-dairy milk in steamers?**
A: Results depend on the device, milk formulation, and technique. Check the manufacturer instructions and milk label; this guide does not provide controlled comparisons across dairy and plant-based milks.

**Q: How hot should steamed milk be?**
A: Optimal serving temperature is 150-155°F (65-68°C). Above 160°F, milk tastes scorched. Below 140°F, it won't create proper foam. Most home steamers reach this range reliably.

**Q: Do I need a separate steamer if my espresso machine has a steam wand?**
A: Not necessary, but backup steamers are useful if your machine breaks. Many enthusiasts appreciate using a dedicated steamer (like the Bellman) while their espresso machine is being repaired or descaled.

**Q: Is latte art possible with automatic frothers?**
A: Difficult because automatic frothers separate foam from milk. Latte art requires integrated microfoam with consistent texture throughout. Manual and wand-based steamers are far superior for latte art attempts.

**Q: How often should I clean my steamer?**
A: After each use, purge steam wands immediately (prevents milk drying). Weekly, run descaling solution through the wand. Monthly, soak disassembled parts in cleaning solution. Annual professional servicing is recommended for espresso machine steamers.

**Q: Can I steam milk in advance?**
A: Not recommended. Steamed milk cools and foam collapses within 5-10 minutes. Milk is best steamed immediately before pouring into espresso.
