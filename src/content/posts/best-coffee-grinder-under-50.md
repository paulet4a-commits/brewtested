## Coffee Grinder FAQ

**Should I buy a burr or blade grinder?**
Burr — every time. Blade grinders chop coffee into random sizes that extract at different rates — producing bitter (over-extracted fines) and sour (under-extracted chunks) in the same cup. Burr grinders crush coffee to a consistent size for even extraction. The OXO Brew at $50 is the minimum burr grinder worth buying.

**Can a cheap grinder make good espresso?**
Not reliably. Espresso requires very fine, extremely consistent grinding — which demands a quality burr grinder (typically $150+). Budget grinders can't produce the precise fine grind espresso needs. For espresso, see our [best burr grinder under $100](/posts/best-burr-grinder-under-100/) for the minimum viable espresso grinder.

**How much should I spend on a coffee grinder?**
As a rule: spend as much on your grinder as you do on your brewer. The grinder affects flavor more than the machine. A $100 grinder + $30 French press beats a $15 blade grinder + $200 espresso machine.

**How often should I clean my grinder?**
Weekly if grinding daily — coffee oils build up and turn rancid. Use the included cleaning brush after each grind, deep-clean with grinder cleaning tablets monthly.

**Does grind size really matter?**
Yes — more than almost any other variable. Coarser grind for French press (longer steep), medium for drip, fine for espresso. Using drip-ground coffee in a French press produces bitter, over-extracted coffee. Using French press grind in a drip machine produces weak, under-extracted coffee.
