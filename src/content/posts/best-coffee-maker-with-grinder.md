## FAQ: Coffee Makers with Grinders

**Q: Is a built-in grinder better than grinding separately?**
A built-in grinder trades some grinding precision for convenience. A conical burr grinder built-in is nearly equivalent to a separate burr grinder, but blade grinders in budget machines can't match separate quality grinders. For daily coffee drinkers, the convenience of one-step grinding usually outweighs the minimal quality compromise.

**Q: How often do I need to clean the grinder?**
Every 1-2 weeks for regular use. Most machines have removable burr carriers that you rinse or wipe. Avoid washing the motor housing itself. Monthly deep cleaning with a brush removes stuck grounds from crevices.

**Q: Can I use dark roast beans in any of these machines?**
Yes, all five machines handle dark roast well. Dark roast beans are slightly more oily but won't damage burr or blade grinders. Just clean the grinder chamber more frequently with darker roasts due to residual oils.

**Q: What's the difference between blade and burr grinders?**
Blade grinders chop beans inconsistently, creating dust and larger chunks. Burr grinders use two rotating surfaces (flat or conical) to crush beans uniformly. Conical burrs are more precise than flat burrs. For coffee maker with grinder, conical burrs (Breville, De'Longhi) provide the best taste.

**Q: Should I store beans in the hopper or in an airtight container?**
Store beans in an airtight container away from light and heat. Use the hopper only for beans you'll grind within a few days—exposure to air in the hopper causes flavor loss. Fill the hopper fresh before brewing for best results.
