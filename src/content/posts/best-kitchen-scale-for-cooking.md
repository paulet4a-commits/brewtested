## Kitchen Scale FAQ

**Do I really need a kitchen scale?**
For baking: yes, absolutely. Volume measurements (cups, tablespoons) vary by 20-30% depending on how you scoop. A scale eliminates this variability entirely. For savory cooking, it's less critical but still useful for portion control and recipe scaling.

**What precision do I need?**
- Baking: 1g is sufficient for home baking. 0.1g is overkill unless you're doing pastry-level work.
- Coffee: 0.1g helps for small doses — see our [best coffee scale under $50](/posts/best-coffee-scale-under-50/) guide.
- Cooking: 5g precision is plenty.

**What capacity do I need?**
Most home cooks need 5-11lb. Go 17lb+ if you regularly cook in large batches, make jam/preserves, or bake multiple loaves.

**Can I use a coffee scale for cooking?**
Yes — especially the OXO Good Grips or Jennings CJ4000 from our [coffee scale guide](/posts/best-coffee-scale-under-50/). Most kitchen scales also work for coffee with one caveat: they respond more slowly than dedicated coffee scales, which matters for pour over but not for baking.

**How do I clean a kitchen scale?**
Wipe the platform with a damp cloth. Never submerge the scale body in water. Scales with removable stainless platforms (like the OXO) can have the platform washed separately.

**Are cheap kitchen scales accurate?**
Yes — at this price tier, accuracy is not the differentiator. The Ozeri at $15 measures as accurately as scales 5x the price. What you pay more for is build quality, capacity, and convenience features.
