French press brewing is forgiving, bold, and delicious—if you use the right coffee. The immersion method requires different beans than pour over or espresso. Here's what works, what doesn't, and exactly which coffees We've researched and compared and trust.

## Why French Press Needs Different Coffee

French press steeps grounds directly in hot water for 4 minutes. This extended contact extracts oils and sediment that other methods filter out. Your coffee choice matters more here than anywhere else.

**The catch:** French press doesn't have a paper filter. That metal mesh lets coffee oils and fines through. Lighter roasts can taste muddy and over-extracted. Dark roasts cut through the body and stay clean.

**The sweet spot:** Medium to dark roasts with low acidity. You want coffee that tastes bold, not bitter.

## Coarse Grind: Non-Negotiable

Before we talk beans, let's talk grind. French press demands **coarse grind**—like breadcrumbs or sea salt. Fine grounds cloud the cup with sediment and over-extract in 4 minutes.

The ratio is simple: **1 gram coffee to 15 grams water**. For a standard 34oz (1000ml) French press, use about 65g coarse coffee and 1000g water.

**Whole bean vs pre-ground:**
- **Whole bean:** Always better. Grind right before brewing. Use a burr grinder set to coarse. See our [grinder recommendations](/posts/best-coffee-grinder-under-50/) for budget options.
- **Pre-ground:** Works, but loses flavor after 30 minutes of oxidation. Only buy pre-ground if convenience trumps quality for your morning.

## Dark vs Medium Roast: When to Use Each

| Roast Level | Taste Profile | Best For | Examples |
|------------|---------------|----------|----------|
| Light | Bright, acidic, floral | Pour over (not FP) | Complex single-origins |
| Medium | Balanced, chocolatey, clean | French press baseline | Most coffees work here |
| Dark | Bold, smoky, syrupy, low acidity | French press ideal | When you want intensity |

**French press truth:** Use medium or dark roasts. Light roasts can taste hollow and sour in the cup. The oil content and body of darker beans plays beautifully with the metal mesh filter.

## The Brewing Checklist for French Press

Even the best coffee tastes bad if you don't brew right:

1. **Use coarse grind**—sea salt sized, not powder
2. **Heat water to 195-205°F (90-96°C)**—not boiling
3. **Bloom first:** Pour 1cm of hot water, wait 30 seconds, then add remaining water
4. **Steep 4 minutes**—longer for lighter roasts, shorter if too strong
5. **Press slowly:** 30 seconds of pressure, don't force it
6. **Decant immediately**—don't leave coffee sitting on grounds or it over-extracts

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use pour over coffee in a French press?**
A: You can, but it tastes thin and sour. Light roasts are optimized for fast extraction. FP needs darker beans.

**Q: Pre-ground or whole bean?**
A: Whole bean, always. Buy a burr grinder. Pre-ground loses 80% of its flavor within an hour of grinding.

**Q: Should I use filtered water?**
A: Yes. Hard water over-extracts. Filtered or bottled water gives you cleaner, clearer cups.

**Q: Does the brand of French press matter?**
A: Not really. A $15 Bodum and a $60 Espro make nearly identical coffee if you use the same beans and technique.

**Q: Can I use espresso-roasted beans?**
A: Yes, but they're often too dark and oily for FP. Medium-dark roasts are the sweeter spot.
