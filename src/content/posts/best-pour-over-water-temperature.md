Getting your pour over water temperature right is the single biggest factor in extracting great coffee flavor. Water that's too hot burns the beans; water that's too cool leaves them under-extracted and sour. The optimal range is 195-205°F (90-96°C), and a quality gooseneck kettle with temperature control can make hitting this sweet spot effortless.

## FAQ

**What's the ideal pour over water temperature?**  
195-205°F (90-96°C) is the sweet spot for most pour overs. This temperature optimally extracts coffee solids without scorching delicate flavors. If your brews taste bitter, try 3-5°F lower. If sour, go 3-5°F higher.

**Do I really need a fancy kettle, or can I use any kettle?**  
Any kettle will heat water, but temperature control is what separates great pour overs from mediocre ones. A basic gooseneck kettle ($40-60) and a simple thermometer will get you 90% of the way. Premium kettles add precision and convenience, not magic.

**What thermometer should I use to check water temperature?**  
We recommend an instant-read digital thermometer like the ThermoPro (ASIN: B01HU4QVFU). Drop it in your kettle and get a reading in 3 seconds. Fast, accurate, under $15.

**Can I use water that's too hot or too cold for pour overs?**  
Water below 190°F under-extracts, making coffee taste sour and thin. Water above 210°F over-extracts, creating bitter, burnt flavors. Staying in the 195-205°F range is non-negotiable for good coffee.
