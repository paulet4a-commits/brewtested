BRAZIL is the first model to price-check if you want a simple glass press. The manufacturer lists a reusable stainless-steel filter and a borosilicate-glass carafe with a plastic frame. ## Choose Capacity in Ounces, Not Just Cup Names

Bodum counts a cup as approximately **4 oz** in its published instructions. An advertised eight-cup press is therefore not eight full-size mugs. Its 34-oz nominal capacity is about 1 L; finished drink volume is not a promise of a full 1 L serving. [Bodum capacity and use notes](https://www.bodum.com/us/en/1928-57-chambord)

For your own purchase, write down your mug size and how many mugs you want to pour. Compare that need with the maker's usable-fill instructions, leaving space for grounds and the plunger. If you only need a small serving, compare smaller variants and their actual prices instead of assuming the largest press is the better value.

## A Useful Brew Starting Point

Follow the manual for the exact press. Bodum's published directions for these models use coarse grounds, water around **92-96 degrees C**, space below the rim, about **four minutes** of steeping and a slow plunge. These are manufacturer instructions, not a recipe we physically tested. [Bodum brewing directions](https://www.bodum.com/us/en/10938-04b-brazil)

If you prefer a 1:15 recipe as a starting point, **22 g coffee with 330 mL water is a 330 mL batch**, not a full 34-oz press. That is arithmetic, not a measured extraction result. Do not combine that amount with an instruction to fill a 1 L press to the top.

## Before You Spend $30

1. **Check the exact variant.** Match model code, size, material and included filter assembly.
2. **Check spare parts.** Find the correct beaker or mesh before assuming they are interchangeable.
3. **Check cleaning instructions.** Do not assume another model's dishwasher guidance applies.
4. **Check the delivered price.** Include shipping, taxes and any necessary replacement filter or accessories.
5. **Choose by workflow.** Think about serving size, handling, cleaning and storage; a layer count alone does not establish cup quality.
